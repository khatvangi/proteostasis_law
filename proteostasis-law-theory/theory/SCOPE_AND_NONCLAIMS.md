# Scope and Nonclaims

## Scope

The theory links translation strategy to protein-quality-control feasibility through a site-resolved influx and finite rescue resources. It applies to a defined organism, environment, time scale, set of molecular species, thresholds, and initial conditions. Extensions may add oxidative damage, trafficking, compartmentation, phase separation, stress regulation, cell division, or population selection.

## Nonclaims

- Viability is not reduced to a universal scalar mistranslation rate.
- The feasible subset is not the same as the fitness optimum.
- Pareto objectives need not share a universal shape or weighting across taxa or conditions.
- Redundancy does not directly imply error tolerance through a universal formula.
- Saturable rescue alone does not guarantee a fold; influx, aggregation, feedback, and parameter ranges matter.
- The minimal one-variable model does not generate a stable high-burden attractor or hysteresis.
- No numerical bacterial headroom estimate is presently supported here.
- No codon-axis permutation result, triplet-code proof, metal-site enrichment claim, or cross-species geometry is part of the canonical theory.
- Evolutionary positioning near feasibility boundaries is speculative until comparative and mechanistic evidence supports it.

## Claim taxonomy

**Theorem-level:** mathematical results proved from explicit assumptions. **Model consequences:** behavior derived conditionally from chosen kinetics and parameters. **Empirical hypotheses:** measurements that could support or refute correspondence to cells. **Speculative implications:** evolutionary or comparative interpretations not required by the law.
