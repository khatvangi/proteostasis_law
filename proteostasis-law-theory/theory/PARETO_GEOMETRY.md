# Pareto Geometry

Translation cannot generally maximize all desirable properties simultaneously. Faster decoding can consume accuracy margins; proofreading and rescue consume time and energy; broader chemical functionality can increase synthesis or folding demands; allocation to quality control competes with other cellular work. These are candidate trade-offs, not assumed universal pairwise laws.

For attainable objective image `Omega_e = {Phi(u,e): u in U(e)}`, the Pareto frontier is

`P_e = {u: no v exists with Phi_j(v,e) >= Phi_j(u,e) for every j and > for at least one j}`.

Objectives are productive throughput, decoding fidelity, folding yield, functional/chemical diversity, energetic economy, and low quality-control demand. Coordinates require operational definitions; neither dimensional reduction nor a privileged codon axis is built into the theory.

Proteostasis adds a stability filter. The feasible frontier is `P_feas(e) = P_e intersect S_e`, where `S_e` is the preimage of loads and allocations that produce bounded, stable, sub-threshold dynamics. Its boundary may be smooth, folded, disconnected, or uncertain. The geometry is condition dependent because decoding probabilities, expression fluxes, folding loads, resource pools, and thresholds change with environment.

### Consequences under stated models

- Moving along the Pareto surface can cross a proteostasis boundary without a large change in mean error rate.
- Two nearby strategies in conventional sequence or codon summaries can be far apart in site-weighted damage influx.
- Compensation can restore feasibility by reducing influx, increasing effective rescue, reallocating occupancy, or raising clearance.

No cross-species universal geometry is asserted. Establishing shared geometry is a future empirical question requiring comparable measurements and uncertainty.
