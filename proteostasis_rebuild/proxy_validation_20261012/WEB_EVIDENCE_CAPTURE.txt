Here we investigate the effects of isoleucine mistranslation on cellular proteostasis. Isoleucine primarily plays a structural role in proteins, as it is mostly buried in the hydrophobic core of proteins, with a preference for β-sheets [21]. We investigated the effects of proteome-wide Ile substitution with two amino acids that are structural isomers. Nva has a linear side chain and is excluded from protein synthesis. Moreover, it was found in Miller experiments simulating primordial conditions of the early Earth's atmosphere [22], which raises the question of why it was removed from the genetic code in the first place. In contrast, Val is a β-branched proteinogenic amino acid that is highly abundant in the cell [20]. Previously, we have shown that Ile-to-Nva mistranslation is slightly but reproducibly more toxic to E. coli than Ile-to-Val [5].

In this study, we combine a thorough analysis of the proteome response to mistranslation with a proteome-wide quantification of mistranslation. Using high-resolution mass spectrometry, we identified the biological processes most affected by isoleucine mistranslation. To evaluate the structural effects of isoleucine substitution, we analysed DnaK interactors and protein aggregates under mistranslating and non-mistranslating conditions in Escherichia coli. DnaK was chosen as the model chaperone for our experiments because it plays a central role and has the most interactors [14].

We show that Escherichia coli is resilient to unexpectedly high mistranslation levels (up to 20 %). However, such high mistranslation levels threaten the fitness and survival of E. coli, and the bacteria respond by upregulating cellular apparatuses for maintaining proteostasis and utilising the disaggregase ClpB. Long-term mistranslation stress provokes the SOS response and filamentation of E. coli cells.

## Abstract

Accurate pairing of amino acids and tRNAs is a prerequisite for faithful translation of genetic information during protein biosynthesis. Here we present the effects of proteome-wide mistranslation of isoleucine (Ile) by canonical valine (Val) or non-proteinogenic norvaline (Nva) in a genetically engineered Escherichia coli strain with an editing-defective isoleucyl-tRNA synthetase (IleRS). Editing-defective IleRS efficiently mischarges both Val and Nva to tRNAIle and impairs the translational accuracy of Ile decoding. When mistranslation was induced by the addition of Val or Nva to the growth medium, an Ile-to-Val or Ile-to-Nva substitution of up to 20 % was measured by high-resolution mass spectrometry. This mistranslation level impaired bacterial growth, promoted the SOS response and filamentation during stationary phase, caused global proteome dysregulation and upregulation of the cellular apparatus for maintaining proteostasis, including the major chaperones (GroES/EL, DnaK/DnaJ/GrpE and HtpG), the disaggregase ClpB and the proteases (Lon, HslV/HslU, ClpA, ClpS). --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
