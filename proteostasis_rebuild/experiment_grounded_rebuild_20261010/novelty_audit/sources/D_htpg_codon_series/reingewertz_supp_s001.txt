Figure S1. Growth curves of mDHFR variants in the presence of arabinose vs. glucose.
Clones expressing mDHFR variants which appeared to support E. coli growth on plates,
upon GroEL depletion, were sequenced and grown in liquid medium containing antibiotics,
TMP, DAP and either glucose or arabinose. It may be seen that cells expressing the
mDHFR mutants G18S or F32L mutants, for example, grow better in liquid medium
containing glucose vs. arabinose, compared to cells expressing wild-type mDHFR.

Figure S4. Bioinformatic analyses of the nucleotide sequences of DHFR in mollicutes

with or without GroEL. Shown are the average values of CAI (A) and tAI (B) for the

organisms with GroEL (GroEL+, blue plots) and without GroEL (GroEL-, olive yellow

statistical tests used to find significant points (the p-values p1 and p2) are described in the

Figure S5. Effects of synonymous mutations on the levels of expression of mDHFR.
Overnight cultures of MGM100 cells expressing the appropriate mutant and containing
0.2% arabinose, 100 µg/ml ampicillin and 30 µg/ml kanamycin were diluted 1:100, grown
to OD600 of 0.6 at 37 °C and expression was then induced by adding IPTG. Cells were then
grown for another 4 hours at 37 °C, pelleted and samples were then prepared for Western
blotting. The ponceau red staining on the left shows that the samples in the different lanes
correspond to similar numbers of cells. Western blotting of these samples using an anti-
His tag antibody (right panel) was employed to determine the expression levels of the
different mutants. The slightly higher levels of expression of the proteins with the two
synonymous mutations at positions 27 and 129, relative to wild-type mDHFR, is consistent
with codon usage improving protein folding yields. Synonymous mutations at positions
74 and 161, which do not affect GroEL dependence, also lead to somewhat higher
expression levels, thus indicating that release from GroEL dependence is not a simple
function of expression level.
